Two days after the summit I did one small thing that my Build Log should reflect: I corrected the homepage insignia to have the same labeling as the diagram on the [Practice page](/practice). Same shape, same words around it, one page matched to the other. It was perhaps the shortest working session in this log.

The reason it’s worth noting is because once the two pages agreed, I could not stop noticing everywhere else the mark was not aligned, and I started to think through consistency differently. Style guides define consistency. And regularly posted content defines consistency. But maybe most importantly, consistency is defined by the sum of the parts.

I started to think to myself that, try as I might to guide people into my website, nobody encounters my practice all in one place. They meet a profile, then a search result, then maybe the site, in whatever order the internet hands it to them. If every landing page hosts different arguments but the elements are not the same (or at least too far away from each other from a UX perspective), the visitor may not conclude I’m versatile. They may conclude nothing, and leave.

A mark on a website is a favicon. A mark that appears the same way, with the same words next to it, on every surface where a person might meet me and my content is a brand. It is the discipline of making one claim, in one vocabulary, in more than one place at a time. It’s a communication layer above the style guide.

So the eighth was not a build day necessarily. It was a day marked by making my argument consistent. I am a designer, an engineer, and a leader.

Before I committed to the practice of building a log of my experience, I knew that it would lead me to tell a story that was hard to follow. I have not had a consistent voice in the digital world since graduate school, circa 2014. The story I decided to tell was fresh, but not without its conflicts and complications with time. Part of me wants to catch everyone up to what I've been up to for the past few months, years, etc., but it does me no good to pull people in and leave them in the history. I needed a start that explained the stall - that life happens between the lines, between posts, between the calls, presentations, events. I'm a busy parent. If my story started yesterday to my audience, that's good enough for me. I want people to get a readout of what's happening now.

Writing that start meant going back through my own machines - session transcripts, git history, notes I had left myself, monitoring logs - to rebuild ten weeks I had already lived through. I had not held the details of my working sessions with Claude that closely from day one. I needed the tool to recollect the details for me, which turns out to be one of the most valuable aspects of working this way - the AI interrogates my work, I provide the prose - it reinforces the learning environment I have built up around my work and processes.

## The ramp to outdated content

The Phase 0 Worksheet had held up as the ultimate test of my deep recollection of professional milestones. It was intended to provide backbone and voice to my digital presence. It held me up to old files. The exercise literally drove me to break out old physical hard drives. It helped me answer questions about how far back and under what circumstances I wanted to even start my Log. The log already demanded that I align to a design schema and gated my releases. If visitors would read about my history on day one, no matter what, they may as well feel the tension of how I got here.

## What landed

- **The front of the series** — the prologue and the cases entry, the two holes at the opening, both written to the schema every entry has to satisfy before it will render at all.
- **The prologue would not settle** — several passes in under an hour: recentred off the stall and onto the ecosystem the stall led to, its verdict flipped from the machine generating to me designing, and the Squarespace decision folded in and then moved back out to the entry where it belonged.
- **Interrogation instead of drafting** — the machine held the session record and asked the questions; the prose came back the other way. Nothing went up that I had not written.
- **Voice gating** — the entries already public were passed over from the top and rewritten until they read as mine. One was retitled on the way through and kept its address.
- **The index started saying what was inside** — every entry announces itself on the landing page instead of sitting in a list of titles, the run reads end to end, and two more went up the same afternoon to make six.

## Breaking the shell

My departure from Squarespace was so fundamental to the work I had produced since I mustered the energy to actually author my story. I broke out of that shell like the metaphorical crab reaching capacity at the size of its shell. The process I took to give the new website life took weeks and the voice I crafted was born into it. I just needed time to gather my thoughts, recollect the events, produce content worth reading.

Going back over all the entries I had already published without having previously invited readership was the hill I decided to die on, so to speak. The facts, the structure, the architecture - all of these elements of my work were true, holding for two striking realities: any time the AI produced drafts of my work, as end products, they were hollow and directionless; and while it had produced reliable summaries of my activity, it could not reconstruct a meaningful narrative for me. To offload this cognitive load was never the point. Instead, I built a process with the AI to repeatedly set me up with the output of our session activity and then interrogate me for prose. That way, I connect the dots in my own words.

Each successive log entry is more of my own story, told by me.

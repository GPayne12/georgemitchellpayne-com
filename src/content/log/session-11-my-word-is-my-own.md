I've been working with Claude and Gemini daily for the past few months, mostly Claude, because of the way I integrated it into my systems. I work through files, tasks and projects with AI. I build applications and learning solutions with AI. I rely on it primarily for the work I cannot or will not do, given the time frame I have to operate in. People all over the world seem to be grappling with that same thing regardless of the tools they use.

Some valuable examples of AI-assist: I connected all my devices over SSH using Tailscale. I built this website to sustain a rapid domain name shift and content restructuring. I deployed a School Drop Shortcut from my iPhone to my desktop. The examples go on and on.

It is not that I am incapable of deeply learning these things. But I'm a busy parent. I have come to rely on the raw building power of AI more than anything else. I do not believe AI has made me smarter. But it has had a multiplying effect on my work, like increasing the number of arms I have available to type at any given time.

In some ways the research about daily AI use is deeply troubling. But in some ways this is the moment many of us were waiting for. The earliest commercial for Codex dramatized the feeling of "lift off" for people working at their computers. That feeling is very relatable.

## The one thing I hold

That said, the one thing I hold the most control over is the voice authoring the entirety of my digital presence. It is not, and perhaps never will be, valuable to me to have a robot speak for me. I don't need a surrogate. I need assistance.

Some people question their own written word because it does not read the way they feel, or does not convey meaning the way it resonated inside. But I have always liked to write. AI may add milestone markers to my canvas, but my word is my own. To this day it feels good to put pen to paper. It slows my thoughts down to the speed of my pencil, and the connectivity to my own writing is the reward unto itself.

## Two things complicate it

First: I can't write fast enough. My poor hands, typing at some slightly above average speed, cannot keep up with my brain. This must be true for others, but because I'm intentionally leaving a written record it really bothers me. There is a lot going on, and distilling a thing down to its essence is deliberate, additional work. Sometimes I forget things as I write, and only after publishing do I recognize the missed opportunities.

Second: I don't understand everything. When it comes to technical implementation using AI, there are gaps, and it is why I have gone to such great lengths to read every line of chat transcript and approve edits manually in this first year. Working in the terminal gave me the confidence to apply for the [MS in AI Engineering](/coursework). But it has not made me fluent in machine language yet.

## AI captures, I provide the prose

So in order to capture and distill the essence of my sessions, I have literally had Claude record and regurgitate the activity for me to parse through and discern narrative. I have undertaken a myriad of AI-assisted tasks and projects, maintenance and security initiatives, web development and design-in-public demonstrations since this past spring. Some people use Obsidian, Notion or Granola to capture their activity and surface the mini-milestones. This has become my preferred method.

AI captures. I provide the prose.

That is not a slogan. It is the specific division of labor that produced the entry you are reading, and this was the week it became a rule.

The rule that settled it is the one I wrote for the LinkedIn series: the post is a doorway, not a summary. Its only job is to earn the click — one concrete detail, one unresolved tension, then the link, and nothing after the link. Everything else on that list is a prohibition against sounding like a machine. No emoji, ever. No Unicode pseudo-bold, which screen readers choke on. No line-break-after-every-sentence. No hashtags. No link-in-comments reach trick, because it breaks the doorway and contradicts a series whose entire premise is directness.

But the rule underneath all of those is the one that matters here: the way to keep a post from reading as generated is not a clever prompt. It is feeding my own log entries back as the voice corpus, and then holding the structure. The entries are the reference for how I sound.

And with the MS in AI Engineering program starting on the 20th, it seemed like the right moment to ground myself in some of the very principles I arrived with — especially integrity, where my word matches my actions.

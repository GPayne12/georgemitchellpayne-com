situation: |
  In late 2024 I was connected to a Program Officer at the UN Office on Drugs and Crime, working with its Global Maritime Crime Programme. He needed to close a specific gap: maritime law enforcement officers of different skill levels needed the basic ability to assess and respond to chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear threats. I am not a nonproliferation expert. The programme paired me with one — a subject-matter expert who owned the technical content — and contracted me to build the instruction around it. The engagement was a home-based United Nations consultancy structured as three deliverables over a contracted 3-month period, with no travel, and no contact with the officers it was being built for.
constraint: |
  The terms of reference scoped the work across a three-month period; the contract was home-based with no travel; and the programme's governance model meant I would not see a classroom, a cohort, or an evaluation result. Every decision I made from the first conversation onward was guided toward sustainability. We worked through a basic contract, on a basic schedule and meeting cadence that kept everyone accountable. There were iterations and pivot points that changed how the deliverable would be constructed. All of these amounted to fairly normal constraints and emergent consideration. That said, the impact of the training would ultimately hinge on its ability to be applied in a variety of contexts. Sequencing, difficulty, and assessment decisions were made without observation and without meeting the instructors who would deliver it. Ultimately, the design had to survive being handed to strangers.
decision: |
  I built it to be run without me. The deliverable set was split deliberately along an access line: the course outline, lecture slides, and assessments go to participants; the learning outcomes, course map, rubrics, and development resources are for instructors and evaluators only. The instructor guide carries a scenario-events list for each table-top exercise so a facilitator can run a session cold. And where a design choice could quietly wreck a schedule, I put it in writing rather than leave it to be discovered — the Kirkpatrick behavior instrument requires a one-to-one conversation with every participant, which on a large cohort materially extends the training, so the handoff tells instructors to plan for it and customize accordingly. Then, after the documentation required to close the contract had been submitted, I delivered one more document that was not requested: a time-demand model putting a range on every activity in the course. I shipped on the contract's terms. I simply delivered an addendum and offered a walkthrough handoff as well.
artifact: |
  Timeline: 2024–2025. Scope: 2 modules · 7 lessons · 9 terminal course outcomes · 10 lecture decks · 27 graded events. Lesson shell, identical across all seven: orientation → the subset of terminal outcomes that lesson carries → an activities list with a time-demand range on every line → instructional content (lecture + deck) → assessments grouped by type → a progress statement that closes the lesson and names the next one. Alignment: each module ships an alignment table crosswalking course objectives × assessments × learning resources, so every assessment traces back to an outcome and every outcome forward to a resource. Outcome coverage widens deliberately across the sequence — 1 outcome in the opening lesson, then 2, then 5 by the close of module 1; module 2's final two lessons carry all nine. Assessment architecture: paired pre-/post-tests (10 items each) bracket the course; three Kirkpatrick instruments — reactions, learning, behavior — run at the open, midpoint, and close, deliberately ungraded at 0% weight; 8 quizzes; 8 discussions, each requiring an initial and a follow-up response; 4 assignments carrying a peer-review criterion; and 2 table-top exercises, one terminal to each module, the second cumulative across both. Weighting: 2,400 points total — quizzes 800, discussions 800, assignments 400, tests 200, table-top exercises 200 — with weight deliberately decoupled from points: the two exercises hold 8% of the points and 40% of the grade. Grading scale: three non-letter bands (Passing 75–100 · Grasping 50–74 · Not Passing 0–49), final determination at instructor discretion. Rubrics: 6 instruments (test, Kirkpatrick, quiz, discussion, assignment, exercise) built from one recurring criterion set — responsiveness to the prompt, demonstration of knowledge, level of engagement and behavior, teamwork, peer review, self-awareness — with engagement and behavior the single largest criterion on the two most heavily weighted instruments (55/100 on discussions, 50/100 on exercises). Time model: every activity carries a range rather than a point estimate; lesson totals run 35–285 minutes and the full learner budget is 685–1,240 minutes, roughly 11–21 hours. Instructor load is modeled separately and explicitly excluded from learner totals — the 1:1 Kirkpatrick assessments alone run 60–120 minutes for a small cohort and 120–240 for a large one. Delivery bundle: course map, syllabus/contract, outline, outcome set, rubric book, time-demand model, instructor guide with per-exercise scenario-event lists, assessment bank, and lecture decks authored inside a client-supplied presentation template.
measuredOutcome: |
  The contract was delivered in full, on the due date with three deliverables across a three-month build: a preliminary assessment and design; a developed course carrying activity documents, scenario storyboards, and assessment instruments; and a complete package of nine finalized learning outcomes, a course map, an outline, a rubric book, an assessment bank, an instructor guide with per-exercise scenario-event lists, and segmented lecture decks. The course was deployed within a month. I know it ran, and I know officers sat in a classroom for it, because someone sent me a photograph of them and the materials on a projection screen. Those photographs are the only outcome data that have reached me since. How many officers, across how many countries, and what the evaluation instruments found all sit with the programme.
engineeringTranslation: |
  Specced and delivered an instructional system, against a three-month contract, and zero access to end users. Built a fixed lesson template instantiated seven times; a many-to-many alignment crosswalk binding nine terminal outcomes to twenty-seven graded events and their supporting resources; a scoring model that deliberately decouples grade weight from point totals; and a parameterized time-cost model expressing every activity as a range with an explicit instructor/learner exclusion boundary. Versioned the outcome set through seven documented revisions before it settled, and staged the build as three contract deliverables. Designed for handoff: the artifact set is partitioned by audience, and the failure mode most likely to break a schedule is documented in the handoff.
